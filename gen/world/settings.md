# Campaign Settings

One section per setting. The **Look** paragraph is meant to be pasted verbatim into image prompts for every event in that place. Use a period note on top of it when the event falls in that period, and a sub-location paragraph when the event happens in one.

Status: canon = the place exists in Eberron canon; campaign-invented = made up for the campaign, with a canon analog used only to fill gaps; campaign-only = made up, no analog. The recap always wins over canon. Line numbers point to `campaign.txt`.

## The Narrator's Library

`narrator-library`. Campaign-only, no canon analog.
Also called: wizard's personal library, the old wizard's library.

**Look.** A dimly lit private library, lined wall to wall with tall bookshelves, not one of them bare. The shelves overflow with texts new and ancient, holy and devilish, and every gap is crammed with trinkets, relics, weapons and curios gathered over countless lifetimes, a museum of lost memories. A heavy desk sits in a pool of flickering candlelight with an ancient clasped book lying open on it. Behind the desk slumps a grizzled, frail old human wizard in green robes, with piercing green eyes and deeply lined cheeks, a pipe drooping from his lips and pipe smoke curling up. He cradles a staff with a raven perched on its top. Beyond the candle's reach the stacks fall into deep shadow. The mood is hushed, ancient and knowing, a storyteller's sanctum outside time.

Sources: recap support rich; recap lines 252, 253, 254, 255, 256, 259, 260, 261, 262, 266, 269, 270; no canon source used.

## DarkWood Prison

`darkwood-prison`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Dreadhold.
Also called: DarkWood, the prison, the Yedani prison.

**Look.** A bleak, secret Brelish military prison hidden in the woods of the Yedani Mountains, reached by sealed prisoner carriages on a rail line. High walls and a heavy gate enclose a yard and a multi-level cellblock of rough fitted stone built around a deep central pit, with a rope-hung elevator platform that drops to the lower levels. Inside are cramped barred cells with stacked bunks, ledges and corridors, a big mess-hall cafeteria with a kitchen, an officers' cafeteria and lounge, the warden's office, a secretary's office, an armory and hidden rooms where a necromancer raises dead soldiers. Inmates wear drab prison fatigues; guards wear Brelish military uniforms and carry handcuffs. Light is dim and institutional, the air stale. The mood is brutal and hopeless, with black-book necromancy hidden underneath.

**Periods**

- From session 1, Intake: Intact and orderly-brutal. New prisoners arrive through the gate to inmates chanting "Fresh fish".
- From session 2, Riot: The riot alarm sounds. Dead guards and prisoners lie in the corridors, rival gangs brawl in the halls and frightened officers barricade themselves in. Zombies climb out of the pit.
- From session 3, Burning and undead: Fire spreads from the secretary's office through the prison and the cafeteria wall is blown out. An undead horde of everyone killed in the riot rises from the floors and the yard. Survivors are trapped on the smoking roof.
- From session 4, Explosion: The fire reaches the armory and the prison explodes. What is left is a burning, smoking ruin inside its walls, with zombie corpses heaped in the yard.

**Sub-locations**

- **Prisoner train carriages** (`prisoner-carriages`). Two sealed, windowless prisoner carriages on a train, pitch dark inside and packed with prisoners in drab fatigues slumped against the walls. When the train lurches to a stop and a door swings open, blinding daylight floods in on squinting faces. Outside, the line runs on into the woods toward the high walls of the prison.
- **Prison gate and walls** (`gate-and-walls`). A heavy prison gate set in high stone walls at the edge of the woods. On arrival, inmates crowd behind it chanting "Fresh fish" at the newcomers. At the end it is the way out: guards swing it open to send help, and the prison entrance beside it belches flame and smoke as survivors fight their way out past zombies.
- **Cells and bunks** (`cells-and-bunks`). Cramped cells of rough fitted stone behind iron bars, each with two-tier bunks for prisoners in drab fatigues. One loose rock in a wall hides a small cavity. The cell blocks open onto narrow ledges and corridors above the lower levels. During the riot, sleeping or captured guards end up locked inside the empty cells.
- **Prison cafeteria** (`cafeteria`). A large mess hall of tables and benches next to the prison kitchen, with water buckets by the kitchen door. It is the scene of every gang fight: overturned tables, bodies on the floor, and finally a whole wall blasted open to the yard outside, with rubble and dust spilling through the hole and the undead pressing in.
- **The pit and its elevator** (`the-pit`). A deep, dark shaft at the heart of the prison, open to the stone levels around it. A rope-hung elevator platform rises and falls inside it. Undead guards climb out of its depths and grab at anyone near the edge; when its ropes are shot through, the platform and everyone riding it plunge back down into the dark.
- **Officers' cafeteria and lounge** (`officer-cafeteria-and-lounge`). The officers' own dining room and lounge, a step up from the inmates' mess hall but still the same bleak stone. During the riot frightened young officers barricade themselves inside behind piled furniture. A connecting way leads from it through to the warden's office.
- **Warden's office** (`wardens-office`). The warden's office, reached through the officers' cafeteria: a desk, files and the prison's records, which a nervous record keeper has kept, including the documents of the necromancy experiments. It is where the warden confesses that ex-Brelish soldiers are being turned into zombies for an attack on Karrnath.
- **Necromancy ritual room** (`necromancy-ritual-room`). A hidden ritual room inside the prison where the necromancer raises dead ex-Brelish soldiers as undead troops for the army, a black-book project kept secret even from the High Marshals. It holds necromantic implements, the ritual space and the dead waiting to rise. Bleak stone, dim light, the smell of death.
- **Secret inventory room** (`secret-inventory-room`). A locked room of confiscated prisoner gear: thieves' tools, trap-disarming kits, pendants, a book, a heavy armored suit and a holy symbol, piled on shelves. In one corner stands a tall mirror covered by a tarp; uncovered, it sends nightmares into anyone who looks at it. A friendly mimic lurks here disguised as a chest.
- **Prison yard** (`prison-yard`). The open yard inside the prison walls, reached by rappelling down through the blown-out cafeteria wall. The dead claw up out of the ground here and shamble toward anyone still alive, while the burning cellblock looms above and flames spread along its windows.
- **Prison roof** (`prison-roof`). The flat stone roof of the cellblock, the last refuge of guards, prisoners and gang members fleeing the undead. Smoke and fire rise around its edges as zombies climb up from below. Survivors are lowered from it to the yard by magic.

## Mara'bor

`marabor`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Mror Holds mines (Kundarakhold / the Realm Below).
Also called: Marabor, the hidden mountain village.

**Look.** A hidden village inside a mountain, home to the families of the prison's guards and officers. It is entered through a guarded mine entrance and reached by a wild mine-cart ride down a steep track into the rock. The houses stand in a cave beside a river that runs through the village: the warden's house, a guard captain's house, a family bar, a guard's house with a secret door, and an abandoned house with boarded doors and windows over a hidden cellar. The houses are dwarven-cut stone with windows, lit by dim lamplight, and unlit side shafts run off into the dark. Residents are off-duty Brelish soldiers and their wives and children. A gelatinous cube roams the tunnels. The mood is furtive, closed and watchful, a company town hiding a necromancer.

**Periods**

- From session 4, Inhabited: Families at home, guards at the cave mouth, horses and carriages waiting outside the mine.
- From session 5, Abandoned: After the night at the abandoned house, every family has packed up and left. The houses stand empty and dark, and outside the mine the horses and carriages are gone.

**Sub-locations**

- **Mine entrance** (`mine-entrance`). The mouth of a mine cut into the mountainside, the only way into Mara'bor, with two guards posted at the cave entrance. Outside it, horses and carriages wait on the rough ground. Inside, a mine-cart track drops steeply away into darkness.
- **Mine-cart ride** (`mine-cart-ride`). A rattling mine cart racing down a steep track through the rock into the heart of the mountain, its riders packed in and holding on, rough tunnel walls blurring past in near darkness, until the track levels out in the dim cave of the hidden village.
- **Elric's house** (`elrics-house`). A guard's modest stone home in the village, where a hot meal is served to escaped prisoners in the small hours. Later it is ransacked, drawers out and furniture tossed. At the back of a closet a secret door opens on a hidden space big enough for a wounded man or two bound guards.
- **The warden's house** (`sharpe-house`). The late warden's home in the cave village, kept by his old, sad widow, who serves tea while strangers search the rooms. Among his personal effects are a painting of his family and his son's suicide note. Quiet, tidy, dimly lit and grieving.
- **Bloodworth Bar** (`bloodworth-bar`). The village bar, run by the wife of the brutal prison officer Bloodworth with her two children about. It is seen only from outside, through the window: a small lamplit family taproom of stone and wood tucked among the houses of the cave village.
- **The abandoned house** (`abandoned-house`). An empty house with its front door barred and boarded and its back window boarded over. Inside it is trashed, dusty and dark, with a hatch in the floor leading to a cellar. It becomes a hiding place, a stakeout and a midnight ambush site.
- **Grail's cellar** (`grails-cellar`). Under the abandoned house's trapdoor: a one-room living space done in black and purple, crammed with necromantic items. On the walls and table are a painting of the Grimdell family when younger, the sigil of House Thuranni, a map of the Yedani Mountains marked with five prisons, and a gothic sending stone.
- **Village river** (`village-river`). A river running through the cave village between the houses, narrow enough to jump across at a run, a dark ribbon of water dividing one side of Mara'bor from the other under the dim cave light. Stone houses with lit windows stand on both banks, and the sound of the water fills the cave.

Sources: recap support some (materials and light from the Mror analog); recap lines 658, 662, 680, 686, 687, 721, 827, 842, 853, 857, 861, 885, 954, 958; gap fill: dwarven-cut stone, windows, dim lamplight and unlit shafts from the Mror Holds mines; canon <https://eberron.fandom.com/wiki/Mror_Holds>, <https://eberron.fandom.com/wiki/House_Kundarak>, <https://keith-baker.com/mror2/>.

## Yedani Mountains

`yedani-mountains`. Campaign-only, no canon analog.
Also called: the mountain, the valley, Yedani Valley, the mountain valley.

**Look.** Rugged forested mountains under an overcast sky, their peaks wrapped in mist. Steep wooded slopes break into rock cliffs and open fields above a deep valley floor. A fast river rages through the woods to the lip of a high waterfall. The forest hides tripwire traps rigged with crossbows and flare arrows, bear traps, and pit traps that drop into caves. Dire wolves, harpies and an owlbear hunt here. Secret Brelish prisons and a hidden mining village are tucked into the range. Aundairian soldiers and hobgoblin squads patrol from a makeshift airship base on the valley floor, signalling with red and green flare arrows, while harpies scan the cliffs. The mood is wild, tense and exposed.

**Periods**

- From session 5, Hidden Sentinel camp: The Kingsblade sits hidden under a huge illusion in a clearing, with tents pitched below it.
- From session 6, Aundairian base: An Aundairian Broadsword and Broadaxe sit at the bottom of the valley in a makeshift base with an Aundairian camp around them.
- From session 7, Broadaxe down: The Broadaxe has crashed to the valley floor after its elemental cell exploded. An enraged smoke elemental rampages through the camp.
- From session 8, Second prison breaks: Another prison under the mountain explodes and zombies pour out across the slopes while airships fire down on them.

**Sub-locations**

- **Forest road under the clouds** (`road-under-the-clouds`). A forest road leading away from Mara'bor, walked on foot under a low overcast sky. A rumbling like a stampede rolls in, something like a huge bellowing monster screams overhead toward the mountain, and blue and red lights flicker deep inside the clouds.
- **Hidden clearing camp** (`hidden-clearing-camp`). An empty-looking clearing in the woods about forty minutes from Mara'bor. Soldiers appear and vanish as they step through a massive illusion. Past the threshold, a humongous airship fills the clearing with a gangway lowered to the grass and military tents pitched beneath its hull.
- **Aundairian valley base** (`aundairian-valley-base`). A makeshift Aundairian staging base on the valley floor, seen from the slopes above: two airships, the lighter Broadsword and the heavy Broadaxe, parked on the ground among a camp of Aundairian soldiers, hobgoblins and harpies, with a waterfall dropping from the cliffs nearby.
- **Forest trail and traps** (`forest-trail-and-traps`). Dense mountain forest on the way down to the valley. A dead dire wolf and a dying Aundairian soldier lie on a blood trail; tripwires strung between trunks trigger crossbows and flare arrows, and bear traps hide under the leaf litter. Squads of hobgoblins move between the trees.
- **Cliff and open field** (`cliff-and-open-field`). A sheer rock cliff rising above an open field in the mountain forest, with a fallen log leaned against it as a makeshift ramp. A harpy guards the top of the cliff and an Aundairian archer watches the field below for anyone trying to climb, ready to fire a flare.
- **Owlbear cave** (`owlbear-cave`). A hidden pit trap in the forest floor, covered over until someone steps on it, dropping into a dark cave below that is the lair of an owlbear. Only a rope tied to a tree at the lip of the hole leads back up into the light.
- **River and waterfall** (`river-and-waterfall`). A fast, raging river crossing the forest, white water racing over rocks toward the edge of a tall waterfall. A rope is strung across it for crossing. From the lip of the falls, the airships in the valley camp below are close enough to leap onto.
- **Second mountain prison** (`exploding-mountain-prison`). Another secret prison built under the mountain, bursting apart in an explosion. Zombies pour out of the wreckage across the slopes with no guards, prisoners or necromancer in sight, while two warship airships hover overhead and fire down on the horde.

Sources: recap support rich; recap lines 737, 961, 963, 966, 1085, 1134, 1147, 1152, 1174, 1177, 1239, 1255, 1259, 2660; no canon source used.

## The Kingsblade

`kingsblade`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Elemental airship / Lyrandar stormship.
Also called: Kingsblade, the Galifar Class ship.

**Look.** A brand-new, humongous Brelish Galifar-class war airship, the Sentinels' flagship. An open top deck crowns several decks below: an engine hull where dwarf and duergar engineers keep the elemental engines and machinery running; a lower deck where halfling pilots service small Shuriken fighters beside the armory; a main deck where orc crew haul machinery; crew quarters and a mess where gnome cooks serve food; a war room; and a bridge. It carries guns, fires docking cables to haul in smaller ships, and is fast at full speed. It has a soarwood hull, a bound elemental ring held on arms at the stern, and a ship's-wheel helm. Crew wear black Sentinel uniforms, and the bridge has a jolly, piratical captain. The mood is proud, busy and military.

**Periods**

- From session 5, Maiden voyage: Parked in a clearing in the Yedani Mountains under a huge illusion, tents pitched below it, then launching on its first voyage.
- From session 8, Home at Hobbstweedle: Runs at full speed for the first time, then sits on the lawn of the Hobbstweedle airfield beside the Broadsword, with a crane alongside.
- From session 13, Hijack night: The gangway guards lie dead and a dead duergar engineer and a dead deep gnome chef are inside. The ship lifts off, then drops back onto the airfield lawn.
- From session 14, Rebuild: Dwarves are rebuilding its elemental engines.
- From session 18, Departure: Leaves Hobbstweedle with the party's friends to fight a Karrnathian airship fleet.

**Sub-locations**

- **Engine hull** (`engine-hull`). Deep in the hull, dwarf and duergar engineers work among the machinery and elemental engines that keep the ship flying. It is hot, loud and crowded with machinery and tools, overseen by the dwarf head of engineering, a place an engineer could happily spend all day.
- **Lower deck hangar and armory** (`lower-deck-hangar-and-armory`). A lower deck hangar where halfling pilots tinker on their small Shuriken fighters and launch them out for scouting. The armory is on the same deck, with racks of weapons and armor and stacks of black Sentinel outfits issued to new recruits.
- **Main deck** (`main-deck`). The broad main deck of the warship, busy before launch: orc crew move heavy machinery around, crates and gear are lashed down, and Sentinels in black uniforms hurry past to their posts. Below are stairs to the inner decks; above is open sky or, in a clearing, the shimmer of the camp's illusion.
- **Bridge** (`bridge`). The command bridge, with the ship's controls and helm, run by a jolly, pirate-talking captain and his bridge crew, among them a shifter. Windows look out ahead of the ship. From these controls the whole warship can be lifted off the ground, and on a bad night it becomes a battlefield.
- **War room** (`war-room`). The ship's war room, where the major briefs his teams with maps and scouting reports: the enemy captain who calls him his archrival, the two Aundairian ships in the next valley, and the smokepowder satchels issued to blow an elemental's holding cell. Military, focused and lamplit.
- **Crew quarters and mess** (`crew-quarters-and-mess`). Crew quarters with bunks and a mess where gnome cooks serve meals made for each race. On a good night it fills with Sentinels and freed prisoners eating, drinking, singing songs about the prison fight and gambling with coins, until everyone is drunk.
- **Top deck** (`top-deck`). The open top deck high in the wind, with a view down over the countryside on the approach to Wroat: the city wall with its great guns, Brokenblade Castle and the obsidian Citadel on the river island, and a small forest outside the city where the ship comes down.
- **Gangway and docking cables** (`gangway-and-docking`). The ship's lowered gangway, guarded by Sentinels whenever it is parked, and the docking cables it fires in flight to grab and haul a smaller ship in alongside. On the Hobbstweedle airfield a crane stands next to its hull, tall enough to climb up to the decks.

## The Moonwatch

`moonwatch`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Lyrandar stormship / elemental airship (canon Moonwatch is a town, not a ship).
Also called: Moonwatch, the Broadsword, the Windshire, the ship.

**Look.** A captured Aundairian Broadsword-type airship, now the party's own Sentinel ship. It is lighter and faster than a warship, built for docking onto and boarding other ships, with light armor and mounted guns. An open top deck, the flight deck, carries the pilot's controls; stairs lead below deck to crew cabins, a galley, storage rooms and a lower deck of crates. It sets down in fields on landing gear, with a hatch in the gear that people sneak through. Its engine has a compressor for a boost. It has a soarwood hull, a bound elemental ring on arms at the stern and a ship's-wheel helm. The crew is a ragtag family in modified black Sentinel uniforms: halfling pilots, gnome cooks, drow, dwarves, a shifter, rescued civilians and children. There are no mirrors aboard.

**Periods**

- From session 6, Aundairian Broadsword: Still an Aundairian warship, crewed by Aundairian soldiers, hobgoblins and a hobgoblin warlord, with harpies around it. It is seized in a fight on its deck as it climbs the mountain.
- From session 8, Renamed: After landing in the Boranex forest it is renamed from the Windshire to the Moonwatch. It parks at the Hobbstweedle airfield beside the Kingsblade.
- From session 18, Christened and upgraded: Recrewed for the party's first mission, with an upgraded compressor. Every mirror is thrown overboard except one makeup mirror hidden inside the mimic.
- From session 29, Battered: Holed and scorched by the anti-airship guns of the armed lightning rail.
- From session 30, Armored: Repaired from aft to bow and fitted with Brokk Broadhelm's armor plating.
- From session 35, Zenya's banner: A garish pink banner with a rhinestone seal of Breland and the slogan "We Don't Die" now flies on board. Below deck there is a makeshift chapel to Dol Arrah built from odds and ends.

**Sub-locations**

- **Flight deck** (`flight-deck`). The open top deck and flight deck in the wind, with the pilot's station at the controls, a rail at the edge and the sky beyond. Fights, landings and visiting dragons happen here; on calm days children play and take turns at the controls, and a shuttered signal lamp flashes messages to the ground.
- **Pilot's controls** (`pilot-controls`). The helm station on the open deck, where the halfling pilot banks the ship hard enough to throw boarders over the rail, pulls braking maneuvers to shake pursuers, and holds course while the navigator plots the route. Wind, cloud and the ground far below fill the view around it.
- **Crew cabins** (`crew-cabins`). Small private cabins below deck, each made personal with bunks, books and clutter. One becomes a nursery for rescued shifter children, another a quiet room where a revived woman recovers, another a sickroom where a possessed mother lies tied to the bed.
- **Lower deck and storage** (`lower-deck-and-storage`). The lower deck of crates and supplies in the belly of the ship, with storage rooms where a prisoner can be tied up under guard, art supplies spread out for children's crafts, and crates to kick in a temper. Dim, cramped and creaking.
- **Landing gear hatch** (`landing-gear-hatch`). The ship's landing gear resting on the grass of a landing field, with a hatch through it into the hull. It is the quiet back door of the ship, used at night to slip aboard or sneak out unseen while someone keeps watch on the main gangway.
- **Engine and compressor** (`engine-and-compressor`). The ship's engine space and its compressor, which can boost the ship for up to an hour twice a day while the engineer watches the engine the whole time. Pushed harder than it should be, it gives enough speed to knock a dragon aside.
- **Galley** (`galley`). The ship's small kitchen, run by a proud gnome cook: dinners for the whole crew, rival batches of cookies baked in the night, pots of tea, and a ridiculous hand-built tea machine whose brew makes drinkers hear squirrels insulting them.
- **Makeshift chapel** (`chapel-to-dol-arrah`). A makeshift chapel to Dol Arrah below deck, built by the ship's cleric out of random odds and ends from the ship's stores. She holds services here, even for the ship's animals, a dog and a pigeon among them. It is a homemade altar for a homemade congregation.

## The Broadaxe

`broadaxe`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Elemental airship.
Also called: Aundairian Broadaxe.

**Look.** An Aundairian Broadaxe-type airship: a heavily armored, slower gunship built to deal maximum damage, its sides bristling with cannons. A big rotor with sweeping blades sits on its upper works, and deep inside an elemental holding cell binds a smoke elemental that powers it. It rests on the floor of a mountain valley inside a makeshift Aundairian base, crewed by Aundairian soldiers and hobgoblins, with harpies circling. It has a soarwood hull and a ship's-wheel helm. It is the heavy partner of the faster Broadsword, and the two lift off together. The mood is heavy, menacing and industrial.

**Periods**

- From session 7, Destroyed: As it lifts off, a smokepowder charge blows its elemental holding cell apart. It falls back onto the valley floor as wreckage, and an enraged smoke elemental bursts out, attacking everyone nearby.

**Sub-locations**

- **Rotor and upper works** (`broadaxe-rotor`). The top of the armored Broadaxe around its big rotor, where the blades sweep past at speed above the cannon-lined hull. An invisible saboteur leaps onto the rotor housing from the cliffs and plants a fused smokepowder charge, with the valley camp and the waterfall below.

## The Kunai

`kunai`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Elemental airship.
Also called: Kunai dropship, the dropship.

**Look.** The Sentinels' Kunai dropship, a small, fast transport flown by a former dragon-riding pilot. It bursts out of mountain mist to drop a squad of reinforcements onto an enemy ship's deck mid-flight, and it also ferries officers to meetings with the High Marshals. It has a light soarwood hull, a small bound elemental ring at the stern and a ship's-wheel helm, and its topside crew wear smoked goggles and heat-resistant clothes. The mood is quick, daring and last-minute.

## Shuriken Fighters

`shuriken-fighters`. Campaign-invented, with a canon analog: Elemental airship.

## Inside the Bag of Devouring

`bag-of-devouring`. Campaign-only, no canon analog.
Also called: the bag, the bag of holding, the Astral sea.

**Look.** The inside of a bag of devouring: a dark extradimensional pocket where nasty tentacles drag in whatever reaches inside. Through its mouth is a portal, and beyond that a ledge over the open Astral Sea, an endless drifting void where anyone who falls floats helplessly away. A dark presence hangs over the space, and inside it the Dreamweaver presses on sleepers' minds. Lost gold and loot lie somewhere inside, and baby morkoths crawl out of it, leaving water behind them. The void is dim and directionless. The mood is claustrophobic, alien and hungry.

**Periods**

- From session 5, Devouring: Tentacles drag a man inside and eat him, and the bag burps.
- From session 12, Dreamweaver's door: Used as a trap to drag sleepers in and possess them. One man is shoved off the ledge into the Astral Sea.
